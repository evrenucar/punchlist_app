The day strip

Not a list of everything. Three answers to three questions you actually ask, in one line at the top of the board.

   NOW              NEXT              SLIPPED
   ───────────────  ────────────────  ─────────────────
   Write the        Call Sam          Renew registration
   release notes    in 40 min         from yesterday
   started 12 min
  • NOW is what you said you would be doing at this moment. It is the answer to "I lose the block".
  • NEXT is the only thing you need to see coming, and how long you have. It is the answer to "I remember late, at 11pm".
  • SLIPPED is what was meant for earlier and did not happen. It is the quiet line you asked for, and it replaces the popup you did not want.

The strip is a view over tasks that already have a time. It invents nothing and it stores nothing new.

Recurrence, which is the part with real weight

You named two shapes and they behave differently:

  DAILY HABIT                          STANDING MEETING
  "read 30 minutes"                    "Monday review, 10:00"

  ticking it today must not            it is the same event every week,
  delete tomorrow's                    and skipping one does not
                                       cancel the series
  ─────────────────────────            ─────────────────────────
  one task, many days                  one series, many instances
  history matters (streak)             history barely matters

The trap: if a repeating task is one row that you tick, then ticking it destroys the thing that has to come back. Every task app gets this wrong at least once.

The honest model is that a repeating task is a rule that produces instances, and the rule is what you edit while the instance is what you tick.

   rule:  "read 30 minutes" · every day · 21:00
             │
             ├── Mon 28  [x] done
             ├── Tue 29  [ ] today   ← this is the row on your board
             └── Wed 30      not created yet

Only today's instance exists on the board. Yesterday's stays done, tomorrow's appears tomorrow. Ticking never touches the rule.
